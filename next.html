Help

    

      
Kurt is a small language for mathematical proofs: one claim per line, and Kurt checks
      each one and says why it follows. Everything runs here in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

      
This is the new editor: the proof is checked while you type, and the results are in the
      editor itself. The classic playground shows Kurt's output beside the editor, and has the
      shell; both share your draft.


      
Reading the results

      
    
        
  • Each checked line has its reason at its end, in grey: ; by 3(4) says that
        the line follows by the rule of line 3 from the fact of line 4.
    • 
        
  • A line that doesn't follow is underlined in red, a todo in yellow; their
        numbers are marked too. Problems, below the editor, lists them all; tap one to go there.
    • 
        
  • Hover over a line (or put the cursor on it: the bar below the editor) to see what Kurt
        says about it: the error, or how the line follows -- the rule and the facts it uses, marked in the editor.
        Tap the bar for the details.
    • 
        
  • The header says whether the whole proof is checked.
    • 
      


      
Writing

      
    
        
  • Open the tutorial menu and start with the first lesson; keywords has a
        lesson on each keyword, theories the theories that load brings in.
    • 
        
  • Declare what you use, e.g. bool A, B; use states an axiom, a plain line is a
        claim that must follow. Blocks are indented by four spaces. Comments start with ;.
    • 
        
  • Tab completes at the cursor: on an empty line a next proof step, after = with
        calc on the value.
    • 
        
  • Symbols: tap them in the bar below the editor, or type a LaTeX-style name followed by a space, e.g.
        \forall → ∀, \implies → ⇒, \and → ∧.
    • 
        
  • View: the text size, the reasons on or off and their column, and checking while typing
        (off: Check or Cmd/Ctrl+Enter). Cancel stops a check that takes too long.
    • 
        
  • File: upload or download a .kurt file, or copy a link that contains the proof.
    •
